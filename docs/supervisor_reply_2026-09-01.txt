2. ITEM 11 / THE A8 DECLINE — YOU WERE RIGHT TO DOUBT IT

We went back and checked, as you asked. The decline is not mitigation, and it is
not attack tapering or a seed effect either. It is a measurement artefact.

A8 @60%, 300 s, 5 seeds, two arms varying only enforcement:

    peak UCR   96.0% +/- 4.90 in BOTH arms, identical to three significant figures

The curve reproduces with enforcement OFF, so quarantine cannot have caused it.
The real mechanism: UCR's numerator counted newly distinct (flow_id, packet_id)
pairs, but packet_id is a per-cycle slot index that restarts every cycle, so only
~32 distinct pairs exist in an entire 300 s run. The metric exhausts its
identifier space by about t=100 s and reads zero thereafter. Meanwhile
interception volume PEAKS at t=140-160 s — the attack is strongest exactly where
the published figure shows it defeated.

We have fixed the metric per main.tex: H(p) (SHA3-512, main.tex:1306) as the
packet identity, and |P_total| as the observed-packet population rather than the
demanding-flow injection count. eq:tvr settled the second one — it defines its
set as a predicate over packets, not a flow-size parameter.

Corrected, UCR discriminates by attack intensity for the first time:

    A8 @20%   76.49%        A8 @60%   89.19%

The old A8 caption has to change under any reading. And the honest mitigation
number is lower than the send-side figure suggests: enforcement cuts intentional
duplicate sends by 99.1% (9,950 -> 89) but eavesdropper receptions by only 24.3%
(168,097 -> 127,203), because a quarantined RSU still forwards legitimate traffic
that reaches the eavesdropper. That is your open question 2.3, now with a number:
scheduling-only enforcement leaves about 76% of interception intact.


3. NEW — WHY PER-VARIANT RECALIBRATION WILL NOT FIX A5-A8 FPR

This is the one item that changes a decision you just made. Measured over
1,372,800 rows (32 RSUs, all variants, 4 percentages, 5 seeds).

Two of the eleven LSTM features — d_div and a_tp — are simulation-wide GLOBAL
SCALARS. They carry the same value for all 64 RSUs in every cycle. They are also
the largest-magnitude inputs under hidden forwarding. So in any A5-A8 run every
RSU receives a network-wide "an attack is happening" signal, while ground truth
labels only the attacker. Every other RSU is a structurally guaranteed false
positive. Within-stratum AUC is 0.500 — literally zero per-node information.

This matters for your threshold instruction: theta_HF^(k) = max across
per-variant thetas is the right construction, and we will implement it as
specified. But it cannot fix the A5-A8 FPR, because no threshold can separate
RSUs on a feature that is identical at every RSU. The 73-82% A5-A8 FPR is a
feature-construction defect upstream of any calibration.

Separately, five of the eleven features have exactly zero benign variance, and
fit_scaler substitutes std=1.0 for them, so they enter the network unnormalised
at raw scale while the real-valued features are z-scored.

We would rather you rule on this than have us assume: do we fix d_div/a_tp to be
per-RSU quantities and re-collect, or keep them and report A5-A8 FPR with this
limitation stated? The first is correct but costs a full dataset regeneration.

On your latch reset-boundary flag — noted, and it connects to something we
found. The dominant source of primary-column false positives (95-100%) is a
mismatch between a LATCHED detector and an EVENT-GATED truth. That is not a
tuning problem; it is the same specification question you raised: does a dormant
compromised RSU count as a positive? Once the enforcement fix lands, the
compromised state genuinely ends at quarantine, so moving the latch boundary to
quarantine time would make truth and detector agree by construction rather than
by tuning. We will not move it until you confirm.
